Domain 5: SAFe Lean-Agile Principles

SAFe provides 10 Lean-Agile Principles:

1. Take an economic view
● Deliver early and often
● Deliver value incrementally
● Apply a Comprehensive Economic Framework:-
–Operate Within Lean Budgets and Guardrails
–Understand Solution Economic Trade-Offs: Development expense, Lead time, Product cost, Value, and Risk
–Leverage Suppliers
–Sequencing Jobs for Maximum Benefit: Weighted Shortest Job First (WSJF)

2. Apply systems thinking
● The Solution Is a System and The Enterprise Building the System is a System, Too
–Optimizing a component does not optimize the whole system
–For the system to behave well as a system, a higher-level understanding of behavior and architecture is required
–The value of a system passes through its interconnections
–A system can evolve no faster than its slowest integration point
● Understand and Optimize the Full Development Value Stream
● Only Management Can Change the System

3. Assume variability; preserve options
● Development occurs in an uncertain world
–You cannot possibly know everything at the start
–Requirements must be flexible to make economic design choices
–Designs must be flexible to support changing requirements
–Preservation of options improves economic results
● Apply a set-based approach instead of point-based approach
– Set-Based Design facilitates learning and allows for continuous, cost-effective adjustments towards an optimum Solution

4. Build incrementally with fast, integrated learning cycles
● Apply fast learning cycles
–fast feedback accelerates knowledge
–Improves learning efficiency by decreasing the time between action and effect
–Reduces the cost of risk-taking by truncating unsuccessful paths quickly
–Is facilitated by small batch sizes
–Requires increased investment in development environment
–PDCA = Plan – Do – Check – Adjust, The shorter the cycles, the faster the learning.
● Integration points control product development and reduce risk
–Integration points accelerate learning
–Development can proceed no faster than the slowest learning loop
–Improvement comes through synchronization of design loops and faster learning cycles

5. Base milestones on objective evaluation of working systems
● The problem of phase-gate Milestones:
–They force design decisions too early; this encourages false positive feasibility
–They assume a ‘point’ Solution exists and can be built correctly the first time
–They create huge batches and long queues, and they centralize requirements and design in program management
● Apply Objective milestones instead - PI System Demos - are orchestrated to deliver objective progress, product, and process Metrics.

6. Make value flow without interruptions
● The eight flow accelerators:
-1-Visualize and limit WIP
-2-Address bottlenecks
-3-Minimize handoffs and dependencies
-4-Get faster feedback
-5-Work in smaller batches
-6-Reduce queue length
-7-Optimize time ‘in the zone’
-8-Remediate legacy practices and policies
● Large batch sizes and High utilization increase variability
● Find the optimal batch size to reduce the Total cost = Holding cost + Transaction cost.
– Higher transaction costs make optimal batch size bigger, reducing transaction costs reduces total costs and lowers optimal batch size.
– Higher holding costs make optimal batch size smaller
● Reduce batch size increases predictability, accelerates feedback, reduces rework, and lowers cost
● Long queues are bad queues, they create longer lead times, increased risk, more variability, lower quality, less motivation.
● Little’s Law: Wq = Lq / λ i.e. Average wait time = Average queue length / Average processing rate
– As per Little’s Law, Faster processing time decreases wait and Shorter queue lengths decrease wait
– Control wait times by controlling queue lengths - WIP limits, small batches, defer commitments

7. Apply cadence, synchronize with cross-domain planning
● Cadence
–Converts unpredictable events into predictable occurrences and lowers cost
–Makes waiting times for new work predictable
–Supports regular planning and cross-functional coordination
–Limits batch sizes to a single interval –Controls injection of new work
–Provides scheduled integration points
● Synchronization
–Causes multiple events to happen simultaneously
–Facilitates cross-functional trade-offs
–Provides routine dependency management
–Supports full system integration and assessment
–Provides multiple feedback perspectives

8. Unlock the intrinsic motivation of knowledge workers
● Workers are most qualified to make decisions about how to perform their work
● The workers must be heard and respected for management to lead effectively
● Knowledge workers must manage themselves. They need autonomy
● Continuing innovation must be part of the work, the tasks, and the responsibilities of knowledge workers.
● Unlocking intrinsic motivation with autonomy, mastery, and purpose:
–Autonomy is the desire to be self-directed and have control over what we work on, how we do our work, and who we work with
–Mastery is the urge to get better at what we do and improve our personal and team skills
–Purpose is the desire to do something that matters and has meaning

9. Decentralize decision-making
● Centralize
–Infrequent: Not made very often and usually not urgent (Example: Internationalization strategy)
–Long-lasting: Once made, highly unlikely to change (Example: Common technology platform) –Significant economies of scale: Provide large and broad economic benefit (Example: Compensation strategy)
● Decentralize
–Frequent: Routine, everyday decisions (Example: Team and ART Backlog)
–Time critical: High cost of delay (Example: Point release to Customer)
–Requires local information: Specific and local technology or Customer context is required (Example: Feature criteria)

10. Organize around value
● Value doesn’t follow silos, Instead, organize around development Value Streams

Explanation:-
The 5 Whys is an iterative interrogative technique that uncovers the true nature of a problem by repeatedly asking “why,” with each answer directing the next “why” toward it. This process traces the underlying cause-and-effect chain step by step, moving past surface-level symptoms until a genuine root cause is reached — one that, if addressed, prevents the problem from recurring rather than just patching its immediate effect.

Why the other options are incorrect:
● It allows for assumptions and logic traps — The opposite is true; the technique is specifically designed to avoid assumptions and logic traps by forcing the troubleshooter to trace direct, evidence-based causality rather than jumping to conclusions.
● It is an effective way for the team to collaborate — While group discussion can naturally occur during the process, this isn’t the specific mechanism that makes the 5 Whys effective for root-cause analysis.
● It allows problems of a similar nature to be combined into groups — Describes grouping or clustering techniques like affinity mapping, a different tool from the 5 Whys.

*An AI tool confidently generates a plausible-sounding but factually incorrect answer. What is this risk called?

⬜ Model drift
⬜ Data leakage
✅ AI Hallucination
⬜ Bias amplification

Explanation:-
✔ This is called an AI hallucination — AI creates outputs that seem correct or make sense on the surface but are wrong, due to mistakes in its underlying data or reasoning. SAFe cites the real example of an airline chatbot that confidently invented a discount policy that didn’t exist, leaving the airline liable for it.
✘ Model drift is a model’s performance degrading over time as real-world data diverges from training data. Data leakage is sensitive or private data being exposed, often through third-party AI tools.

Comprehensive list of Free Professional Scrum Master I (PSM I) practice exam questions curated for cracking the Scrum.org PSM I assessment with confidence.

Disclaimer: Scrum.org and the Scrum Guide are protected brands/works. These exam questions are neither endorsed by nor affiliated with Scrum.org, Ken Schwaber, or Jeff Sutherland. These are not the official Scrum.org exam questions/dumps. Every question below is created strictly from the free, official Scrum Guide (the current November 2020 version) and official Scrum.org resources, never from unauthorized dump sites. Scrum.org’s own free Scrum Open practice assessment is another great official self-check resource to try before your real attempt. Once you understand the concepts behind these questions, you are more than ready to crack the real PSM I assessment on your first attempt.

Overview

  1. PSM I validates that you understand Scrum as described in the Scrum Guide and how to apply it in Scrum Teams — the exam tests precise adherence to the Guide’s exact wording, not “common practice” folklore.
  2. The exam consists of 80 questions in Multiple Choice, Multiple Answer, and True/False format.
  3. You have 60 minutes to complete the assessment, and a passing score of 85% is required.
  4. The exam costs $200 USD per attempt; test passwords have no expiration date but are valid for one attempt only.
  5. PSM I certification never expires — it is a lifetime certification with no annual renewal fee, and it includes a free Credly digital credential.
  6. There are no mandatory prerequisites and no required course attendance, though Scrum.org recommends attending a Professional Scrum Master or Applying Professional Scrum course.
  7. The authoritative content source for every question below is the Scrum Guide, current November 2020 version, authored by Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland — there is no newer official revision as of this writing.
  8. Scrum.org does not publish official domain-weight percentages for PSM I (unlike some vendor-specific exams); instead, PSM I questions are drawn from three Focus Areas defined in the Professional Scrum Competencies: Understanding and Applying the Scrum Framework, Developing People and Teams, and Managing Products with Agility.
  9. This guide instead organizes its 50 practice questions around the Scrum Guide’s own structure, since that document is the actual source of truth the exam draws from.
  10. Scrum.org’s own free Scrum Open assessment is an excellent official self-check tool to try before attempting the real exam.
  11. Official PSM I certification page for more details.

Scrum.org does not publish official percentage weights for PSM I. The breakdown below instead follows the Scrum Guide’s own major content sections, weighted by how much text and emphasis the Guide gives each area and how it maps to Scrum.org’s own Focus Areas. Scrum Events and Scrum Team accountabilities receive the most questions since they are both the most detailed sections of the Guide and the areas most consistently emphasized in real PSM I attempts.

Practice Questions

According to the Scrum Guide, Scrum is founded on which two foundations?

✅ A. Empiricism and lean thinking
⬜ B. Waterfall and empiricism
⬜ C. Predictive planning and lean thinking
⬜ D. Agile principles and iterative planning

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide states that Scrum is founded on empiricism and lean thinking, combining an iterative, incremental approach to optimize predictability and control risk.
Why other options are incorrect:
B: Waterfall is a predictive, sequential approach and is not part of Scrum’s theoretical foundation described in the Guide.
C: “Predictive planning” contradicts empiricism, which is based on experience and decisions made from what is observed, not upfront prediction.
D: “Agile principles” is not the phrase the Scrum Guide uses to describe Scrum’s theoretical foundation; the Guide specifically names empiricism and lean thinking.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

Scrum combines four formal events for inspection and adaptation within a containing event. What is that containing event called?

✅ A. The Sprint
⬜ B. The Daily Scrum
⬜ C. The Release
⬜ D. The Program Increment

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide describes the Sprint as the container for all other Scrum events, inside which the four formal inspect-and-adapt events take place.
Why other options are incorrect:
B: The Daily Scrum is one of the four formal events for inspection and adaptation, not the containing event.
C: “Release” is not an event defined anywhere in the Scrum Guide.
D: “Program Increment” is not an event defined anywhere in the Scrum Guide.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

The Scrum Guide names three pillars that uphold every implementation of empirical process control: transparency, inspection, and adaptation. Which TWO of the following statements about these pillars are accurate?

✅ A. Inspection enables adaptation; inspection without adaptation is considered pointless.
⬜ B. Adaptation enables transparency; without adaptation, transparency is impossible.
✅ C. Transparency enables inspection; inspection without transparency is misleading and wasteful.
⬜ D. Adaptation becomes easier when the people involved are not empowered or self-managing.

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide states that transparency enables inspection, and inspection in turn enables adaptation — inspection without adaptation is considered pointless, and inspection without transparency is misleading and wasteful.
Why other options are incorrect:
B: This reverses the actual relationship described in the Guide; transparency enables inspection, not the other way around.
D: This contradicts the Guide, which states adaptation becomes MORE difficult, not easier, when the people involved are not empowered or self-managing.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

Per the Scrum Guide, what happens to inspection when it is not followed by adaptation?

⬜ A. It becomes more valuable
✅ B. It is considered pointless
⬜ C. It automatically triggers a Sprint cancellation
⬜ D. It replaces the need for transparency

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide states plainly that inspection enables adaptation, and inspection without adaptation is considered pointless.
Why other options are incorrect:
A: This is the opposite of what the Guide states; inspection without follow-through adaptation loses its value.
C: The Guide does not connect a lack of adaptation to Sprint cancellation; only the Product Owner can cancel a Sprint, and only if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete.
D: Transparency enables inspection, not the reverse; inspection does not replace or substitute for transparency.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

How many values does the Scrum Guide define, and what is their stated purpose?

⬜ A. Three values; they define the Scrum Team’s roles
⬜ B. Four values; they define the length of each timebox
✅ C. Five values; they give direction to the Scrum Team with regard to their work, actions, and behavior
⬜ D. Five values; they replace the need for Scrum events

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide defines five values — Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, and Courage — and states these values give direction to the Scrum Team with regard to their work, actions, and behavior.
Why other options are incorrect:
A: The Guide defines five values, not three, and values are not role definitions.
B: The Guide defines five values, not four, and values are not timeboxes.
D: Values do not replace the events; the events remain necessary to create the cadence for inspection and adaptation.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

Which TWO of the following are among the five Scrum values named in the Scrum Guide?

⬜ A. Discipline
✅ B. Courage
⬜ C. Predictability
✅ D. Respect

Explanation:
The five Scrum values named in the Scrum Guide are Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, and Courage.
Why other options are incorrect:
A: Discipline is not one of the five Scrum values listed in the Guide.
C: Predictability is an outcome Scrum’s iterative, incremental approach aims to optimize, but it is not one of the five Scrum values.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

According to the Scrum Guide, what is expected of a Scrum Team the moment it learns something new through inspection?

⬜ A. It should wait until the next Sprint Retrospective to act
✅ B. It is expected to adapt immediately
⬜ C. It should escalate the change to the Product Owner for approval before adapting
⬜ D. It should document the learning for the next release cycle

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide states that a Scrum Team is expected to adapt the moment it learns anything new through inspection, so adjustments are made as soon as possible to minimize further deviation.
Why other options are incorrect:
A: Waiting for the Retrospective contradicts the Guide’s emphasis on adapting immediately upon learning something new.
C: The Guide does not require Product Owner pre-approval before a team adapts its own process; scope changes to the Sprint Backlog specifically are negotiated with the Product Owner, but that is a separate point.
D: The Guide calls for adaptation, not a documentation-only response to what was learned.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

True or False: According to the Scrum Guide, artifacts with low transparency can lead to decisions that diminish value and increase risk.

✅ A. True
⬜ B. False

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide states this directly under its description of the Transparency pillar: important decisions are based on the perceived state of the three formal artifacts, and artifacts with low transparency can lead to decisions that diminish value and increase risk.
Why other options are incorrect:
B: This is false — the Guide explicitly states that low-transparency artifacts create exactly this risk.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

Which of the following correctly describes the composition of a Scrum Team as defined in the Scrum Guide?

✅ A. One Scrum Master, one Product Owner, and Developers, with no sub-teams or hierarchies
⬜ B. A Product Owner, a Project Manager, and a Development Team led by a Team Lead
⬜ C. Multiple Scrum Masters overseeing several development sub-teams
⬜ D. A Product Owner and Developers only; the Scrum Master is optional

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide defines the Scrum Team as consisting of one Scrum Master, one Product Owner, and Developers, with no sub-teams or hierarchies within it.
Why other options are incorrect:
B: The Guide does not define “Project Manager” or “Team Lead” roles within the Scrum Team.
C: The Guide specifies one Scrum Master per Scrum Team, and it explicitly states there are no sub-teams within the team.
D: The Guide defines the Scrum Master as one of exactly three accountabilities in the Scrum Team, not as optional.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

What team size does the Scrum Guide suggest a Scrum Team should be to remain nimble yet still complete significant work within a Sprint?

⬜ A. Exactly 5 people
✅ B. Typically 10 or fewer people
⬜ C. Between 15 and 20 people
⬜ D. No size guidance is given

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide states the Scrum Team should be small enough to remain nimble and large enough to complete significant work within a Sprint, typically 10 or fewer people.
Why other options are incorrect:
A: The Guide does not fix an exact number of 5; it offers “typically 10 or fewer” as general guidance, not a hard rule.
C: 15-20 contradicts the Guide’s note that smaller teams communicate better, and that overly large teams should reorganize.
D: This is incorrect — the Guide does give this general size guidance.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

Which TWO characteristics does the current Scrum Guide use to describe Scrum Teams?

✅ A. Cross-functional
⬜ B. Self-organizing
✅ C. Self-managing
⬜ D. Hierarchical

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide describes Scrum Teams as cross-functional, meaning members have all the skills necessary to create value each Sprint, and self-managing, meaning they internally decide who does what, when, and how.
Why other options are incorrect:
B: “Self-organizing” was used in earlier, pre-2020 versions of the Scrum Guide; the current 2020 Scrum Guide specifically uses “self-managing” instead.
D: The Guide explicitly states there are no hierarchies within a Scrum Team.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

Who is accountable for creating a valuable, useful Increment every Sprint, according to the Scrum Guide?

⬜ A. The Product Owner alone
⬜ B. The Developers alone
✅ C. The entire Scrum Team
⬜ D. The Scrum Master, since they own the Definition of Done

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide states that the entire Scrum Team is accountable for creating a valuable, useful Increment every Sprint.
Why other options are incorrect:
A: The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing product value and Product Backlog management, not solely for producing the Increment.
B: Developers do the work of building the Increment, but the Guide assigns the accountability to the entire Scrum Team, not the Developers in isolation.
D: The Scrum Master does not “own” the Definition of Done; it is a shared standard the whole Scrum Team must conform to, and the Guide credits the whole Scrum Team with this accountability, not the Scrum Master specifically.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

Which TWO of the following are accountabilities of the Developers, as defined in the Scrum Guide?

✅ A. Creating the Sprint Backlog, the plan for the Sprint
⬜ B. Ordering the Product Backlog
✅ C. Instilling quality by adhering to a Definition of Done
⬜ D. Setting the price of the certification exam

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide lists Developer accountabilities as: creating the Sprint Backlog, instilling quality by adhering to a Definition of Done, adapting their plan daily toward the Sprint Goal, and holding each other accountable as professionals.
Why other options are incorrect:
B: Ordering the Product Backlog is a Product Owner accountability, not a Developer accountability.
D: Setting exam pricing is not a Scrum concept described anywhere in the Scrum Guide; it is an unrelated distractor.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

According to the Scrum Guide, what is the Product Owner primarily accountable for?

⬜ A. Assigning daily tasks to the Developers
✅ B. Maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team
⬜ C. Facilitating the Daily Scrum
⬜ D. Removing impediments faced by the Developers

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide states the Product Owner is accountable for maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team.
Why other options are incorrect:
A: The Guide describes Developers as self-managing, deciding internally who does what, when, and how; the Product Owner does not assign daily tasks.
C: The Daily Scrum is an event for the Developers; the Guide does not assign its facilitation to the Product Owner.
D: Causing the removal of impediments is listed as a way the Scrum Master serves the team, not a Product Owner accountability.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

Per the Scrum Guide, can a Product Owner delegate Product Backlog management work to others?

⬜ A. No, the Product Owner must personally perform every Product Backlog management activity
✅ B. Yes, but the Product Owner remains accountable
⬜ C. Yes, and accountability transfers to whoever performs the delegated work
⬜ D. No, delegation would violate the self-managing nature of the Scrum Team

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide states the Product Owner may do Product Backlog management work themselves or delegate it to others, but regardless, the Product Owner remains accountable.
Why other options are incorrect:
A: The Guide explicitly permits delegating this work; the Product Owner need not do all of it personally.
C: The Guide states the Product Owner “remains accountable” even after delegating; accountability does not transfer to the delegate.
D: Delegating Product Backlog work does not conflict with the Scrum Team’s self-management, which is about Developers deciding who does what, when, and how within the Sprint.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

According to the Scrum Guide, how should stakeholders who want to change the Product Backlog proceed?

⬜ A. By submitting a formal change request to the Scrum Master
✅ B. By trying to convince the Product Owner
⬜ C. By voting with the Developers during Sprint Planning
⬜ D. By directly editing the Product Backlog themselves

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide states that those wanting to change the Product Backlog can do so by trying to convince the Product Owner.
Why other options are incorrect:
A: The Guide does not describe a formal change-request process routed through the Scrum Master; the Product Owner is the one stakeholders must persuade.
C: Developers do not vote on Product Backlog changes; ordering the Product Backlog is the Product Owner’s accountability.
D: The Product Backlog is a single source of work managed under the Product Owner’s accountability, not something stakeholders edit directly.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

What is the Scrum Master accountable for, per the Scrum Guide?

✅ A. Establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide, and the Scrum Team’s effectiveness
⬜ B. Writing the Product Backlog items
⬜ C. Approving the Increment before it can be released
⬜ D. Managing the organization’s budget for the product

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide states the Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide, and for the Scrum Team’s effectiveness.
Why other options are incorrect:
B: Creating and clearly communicating Product Backlog items is part of the Product Owner’s accountability for Product Backlog management.
C: The Guide describes no Scrum Master “approval gate” for the Increment; in fact, the Sprint Review is explicitly stated to never be a gate to releasing value.
D: Budget management is not described anywhere in the Scrum Guide as a Scrum Master accountability.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

If a Scrum Team becomes too large to remain nimble, what does the Scrum Guide suggest?

⬜ A. Appoint a second Scrum Master to manage the extra members

Why other options are incorrect:
A: The Guide does not describe adding a second Scrum Master to one oversized Scrum Team; each Scrum Team has one Scrum Master.
B: Sub-teams contradict the Guide’s explicit statement that there are no sub-teams within a Scrum Team.
D: Lengthening the Sprint is not the Guide’s suggested remedy for an oversized team; reorganizing the team structure is.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

Which TWO of the following does the Scrum Guide state must hold true during a Sprint?

Why other options are incorrect:
B: The Guide states that scope “may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as more is learned” — it is not completely fixed.
D: The Guide states “The Product Backlog is refined as needed” during the Sprint, so it is not off-limits.
Source: The Scrum Guide (2020)

What is the primary purpose of the Sprint Review, according to the Scrum Guide?

What is the maximum timebox for the Sprint Review in a one-month Sprint, per the Scrum Guide?

C: Eight hours is the Guide’s timebox for Sprint Planning, not the Sprint Review.
D: The Guide does specify a maximum timebox of four hours for a one-month Sprint.

True or False: According to the Scrum Guide, the Sprint Review should be limited to a presentation of the completed work.

The Scrum Guide explicitly states, “The Sprint Review is a working session and the Scrum Team should avoid limiting it to a presentation.”

Which commitment is associated with the Product Backlog, according to the Scrum Guide?

⬜ A. The Sprint Goal
⬜ B. The Definition of Done

Which TWO of the following artifact-commitment pairs are accurate according to the Scrum Guide?

✅ A. Sprint Backlog → Sprint Goal
✅ B. Increment → Definition of Done
⬜ C. Product Backlog → Sprint Goal
⬜ D. Increment → Product Goal

Explanation:
The Scrum Guide pairs each artifact with a commitment: Product Backlog → Product Goal; Sprint Backlog → Sprint Goal; Increment → Definition of Done.

Comprehensive list of AI-Empowered SAFe Advanced Scrum Master (ASM) Exam Questions curated for cracking the exam in first attempt.

Certification update (2026): Scaled Agile now offers this certification as the AI-Empowered SAFe Advanced Scrum Master, recommended for experienced Scrum Masters who already hold the SAFe Scrum Master (SSM) certification. The exam is 60 questions, multiple-choice single-select, 120 minutes, 82% passing score, web-based and closed book. A legacy, shorter “ASM” exam version (20 questions / 60 minutes / 80% passing, covering only 3 domains) also still appears in Scaled Agile’s catalog for candidates on the older learning path — check your Learning Plan to confirm which version you’ve been assigned before you start studying.

Disclaimer: Scaled Agile Inc is a protected Brand. These exam questions are neither endorsed by nor affiliated with Scaled Agile. These are not the SAFe official exam questions/dumps. These questions are created from the public web content of the Scaled Agile Framework, the official ASM exam study guide, and other publicly available Scaled Agile articles. These questions cover all the domains and topics of the official exam and once you go through these questions and their concepts, you are more than ready to crack the exam in first attempt.

Overview

  1. Prepare well for the exam. Understand all SAFe Team and ART flow, coaching, and conflict-resolution concepts covered below.
  2. Requires 1 to 2 weeks of preparation if you already hold SSM and have hands-on Scrum Master experience.
  3. You need to solve 60 questions (multiple-choice, single-select) in 120 mins from your laptop without any supervision. It is an open-resource but closed-book, unproctored online exam — no outside assistance is allowed per the Candidate Agreement.
  4. Passing score is 82%, i.e. you need to get at least 50 out of 60 correct.
  5. Recommended prerequisite: SAFe Scrum Master (SSM) certification and real-world experience facilitating a Scrum team.

Domain 1: Evolving the Advanced Scrum Master Role

What is the primary difference in scope between a SAFe Scrum Master (SSM) and an Advanced Scrum Master (ASM)?

⬜ The ASM writes code alongside the development team
✅ The ASM extends servant leadership and coaching beyond a single team to the ART and cross-team level
⬜ The ASM replaces the Product Owner during PI Planning
⬜ The ASM only facilitates the Daily Stand-up

Explanation:-
An ASM builds on core Scrum Master skills and applies them at a broader scale — coaching other Scrum Masters, resolving cross-team flow and conflict issues, and helping improve ART-level performance, not just team-level events.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

An Advanced Scrum Master notices a team consistently looks to them to make every decision. What leadership behavior should the ASM adopt to correct this?

⬜ Make the decisions faster so the team isn’t blocked
✅ Shift from a directive style to coaching and asking powerful questions so the team builds its own decision-making capability
⬜ Escalate every team decision to the Release Train Engineer
⬜ Assign a technical lead to make decisions instead

Explanation:-
Servant leadership favors growing the team’s self-management capability over the Scrum Master remaining the single point of decision-making.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

Which best describes how an Advanced Scrum Master should use AI tools when coaching teams?

⬜ Let AI fully automate retrospectives and conflict resolution without team involvement
✅ Use AI to augment tasks like summarizing flow data, surfacing patterns, or drafting retrospective prompts, while keeping human judgment and empathy central to coaching decisions
⬜ Avoid AI entirely, since SAFe coaching must remain fully manual
⬜ Use AI only to generate PI Planning slide decks

Explanation:-
“Empowering teams with AI” in the updated ASM curriculum is about augmenting the Scrum Master’s judgment with data and automation, not replacing human coaching and facilitation.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

What is a key responsibility of an Advanced Scrum Master that goes beyond the SSM role?

⬜ Owning the Team Backlog
✅ Coaching other Scrum Masters and helping coordinate Scrum of Scrums across the ART
⬜ Approving the Program Increment objectives on behalf of Business Owners
⬜ Writing the ART’s Vision document

Explanation:-
ASMs often mentor newer Scrum Masters and participate in or facilitate ART-level coordination events such as Scrum of Scrums.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

Which statement best reflects the difference between “managing” and “leading” a team, as emphasized in the ASM role?

✅ Leading focuses on inspiring shared purpose and growing capability, while managing focuses on directing tasks and controlling output
⬜ Leading and managing are identical in SAFe
⬜ Managing means only assigning story points
⬜ Leading applies only to Release Train Engineers, not Scrum Masters

Explanation:-
SAFe’s Lean-Agile leadership principles emphasize leading by example and growing people’s capabilities over command-and-control management.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

A new Scrum Master on the ART frequently asks the ASM to resolve their team’s technical disagreements. What should the ASM do?

⬜ Resolve the disagreement directly since the ASM has more experience
✅ Coach the new Scrum Master on facilitation and conflict-resolution techniques so they can handle it themselves
⬜ Ask the Release Train Engineer to step in

Explanation:-
Part of evolving the ASM role is growing the capability of other Scrum Masters, not solving every problem for them.

Why is continuously growing agile maturity across the organization considered part of the Advanced Scrum Master’s role?

⬜ It is not part of the ASM role; that belongs to the Lean-Agile Center of Excellence only
✅ Because sustainable flow and high performance require the whole organization, not just one team, to keep improving its Lean-Agile practices
⬜ Because the ASM is evaluated only on the number of certifications their teammates hold
⬜ Because it replaces the need for retrospectives

Explanation:-
ASMs act as change agents who help spread Lean-Agile practices and continuous improvement beyond their immediate team.

✅ Flow Velocity
⬜ Flow Load
⬜ Flow Distribution
⬜ Flow Predictability

⬜ Flow Efficiency
✅ Flow Time
⬜ Flow Load
⬜ Flow Distribution

Explanation:-
Flow Time is a customer-centric metric used to answer “are we reducing time to market?”.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/art-flow

⬜ The team has too few work items in progress

⬜ The team’s Flow Velocity is too high
⬜ The Program Increment is too short

Explanation:-
Flow Efficiency is the ratio of active time to total flow time; low efficiency usually points to queues and hand-off delays, not lack of team effort.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/art-flow

Which flow metric helps a team understand whether the amount of work-in-progress matches its capacity?

⬜ Flow Time
✅ Flow Load
⬜ Flow Distribution
⬜ Flow Velocity

Explanation:-
Flow Load tracks all active work items to reveal whether demand and capacity are in balance; too much load causes queues and context switching.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/art-flow

Which flow metric would a Product Owner use to check whether the team is investing appropriately in new features versus defects, technical debt, and risk reduction?

⬜ Flow Time
⬜ Flow Velocity
✅ Flow Distribution
⬜ Flow Predictability

Explanation:-
Flow Distribution shows the proportion of effort allocated across different flow item types (features, defects, debt, risk).
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/art-flow

Which flow metric is most useful for assessing how reliably a Team, ART, or Solution Train delivers against its planned business value at PI boundaries?

⬜ Flow Load
⬜ Flow Efficiency
✅ Flow Predictability
⬜ Flow Time

Explanation:-
Flow Predictability compares actual delivered business value to planned business value, and feeds directly into the ART Predictability Measure calculated at Inspect and Adapt.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/art-flow

Visualizing work and setting WIP limits improves transparency and reduces the cost of excessive multitasking.

Value stream mapping and cumulative flow diagrams are core tools for identifying constraints so they can be addressed directly.

Optimizing “time in the zone” means protecting focused work time and minimizing costly context switching.

Domain 3: Building High-Performing Teams

Which of the following best characterizes a high-performing Agile team?

⬜ A team that never has disagreements
✅ A team with high trust, shared ownership, and a relentless focus on delivering value together
⬜ A team led entirely by a single technical expert
⬜ A team that strictly follows a fixed process without adapting it

Explanation:-
High-performing teams combine trust, transparency, cross-functional collaboration, and continuous improvement rather than rigid process adherence.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/team-and-technical-agility

What does it mean for a SAFe team to be “self-organizing”?

⬜ The team has no Scrum Master or Product Owner
✅ The team decides how best to accomplish the work assigned to it
⬜ The team sets its own compensation
⬜ The team reports only to executive leadership

Explanation:-
Self-organizing teams determine the best approach to achieve outcomes, working within organizational guardrails rather than being told exactly how to work.

⬜ The team sets Portfolio-level strategic themes

Self-managing teams extend self-organization by also having more say in prioritization and sequencing decisions within their guardrails.

In the Forming stage, members are often cautious and avoid conflict as they get to know each other and the work.

Storming is characterized by conflict as team members assert viewpoints and work out roles and norms; it is a normal, necessary stage rather than a failure.

Trust is the base of the pyramid; without vulnerability-based trust, teams tend to avoid healthy conflict and everything above it suffers.

Fear of Conflict leads to artificial harmony, where teams avoid the productive, task-based debate needed to reach the best decisions.

Lack of Commitment often follows unresolved conflict — without a real debate, agreement is shallow and buy-in is weak, so follow-through suffers.

Avoidance of Accountability occurs when team members are reluctant to call out peers on behaviors or performance that hurt the team.

Inattention to Results happens when individual or departmental goals are prioritized over the collective results the team is meant to deliver.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

Psychological safety underlies healthy conflict, honest retrospectives, and continuous improvement — team members must feel safe to speak up.

Powerful questions are a coaching technique that builds the team’s own problem-solving capability instead of the Scrum Master supplying answers.

Domain 4: Addressing Conflict

Which statement best reflects how SAFe views conflict within a team?

⬜ All conflict is destructive and should be eliminated
✅ Task-focused conflict is natural and can be productive, while unmanaged relationship conflict is destructive
⬜ Conflict should always be escalated to the Release Train Engineer
⬜ Conflict only occurs when a team lacks a Scrum Master

Explanation:-
SAFe recognizes that healthy, task-based conflict (debating ideas) improves decisions, whereas personal, relationship-based conflict damages trust and performance.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

A disagreement over which architectural approach to use is an example of what type of conflict?

✅ Task or process conflict
⬜ Relationship conflict
⬜ Value conflict only
⬜ There is no such thing as task conflict in Agile teams

Explanation:-
Task/process conflict concerns the work itself — approach, priorities, or method — and, when handled well, often leads to better outcomes.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

Using the Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Mode framework, which style involves pursuing one’s own position at the expense of the other party, often used in a crisis needing quick, decisive action?

✅ Competing
⬜ Collaborating
⬜ Accommodating
⬜ Avoiding

Explanation:-
Competing is high on assertiveness and low on cooperativeness — useful for urgent decisions, but overused it damages relationships.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

Which Thomas-Kilmann conflict style seeks a solution that fully satisfies the concerns of all parties involved, and is generally best suited to complex issues where the relationship matters?

⬜ Compromising
✅ Collaborating
⬜ Accommodating
⬜ Avoiding

Explanation:-
Collaborating is high on both assertiveness and cooperativeness, aiming for a true win-win, though it takes more time and effort than other styles.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

A Scrum Master and Product Owner each give up part of their preferred approach to quickly reach a workable middle-ground decision before a deadline. Which conflict style does this represent?

⬜ Competing
⬜ Collaborating
✅ Compromising
⬜ Avoiding

Explanation:-
Compromising trades some of each party’s goals for a faster, moderately satisfying resolution — useful under time pressure but not always optimal long-term.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

A team member consistently withdraws from a disagreement and changes the subject rather than addressing it. Which Thomas-Kilmann style is this person exhibiting?

⬜ Accommodating
✅ Avoiding
⬜ Compromising
⬜ Competing

Explanation:-
Avoiding is low on both assertiveness and cooperativeness; it can be appropriate for trivial issues but is harmful if used for important, recurring conflicts.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

A developer repeatedly gives in to a more vocal teammate’s preferences on design decisions to keep the peace, even when they disagree. Which Thomas-Kilmann style does this represent?

✅ Accommodating
⬜ Competing
⬜ Collaborating
⬜ Avoiding

Explanation:-
Accommodating is low on assertiveness and high on cooperativeness — sometimes appropriate to preserve harmony, but overused it can suppress valuable dissent.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

What does “interest-based” (as opposed to “positional”) conflict resolution focus on?

⬜ Determining who has the higher position in the org chart
✅ Understanding the underlying needs and motivations behind each party’s stated position, to find a solution that addresses both
⬜ Always finding a 50/50 compromise regardless of underlying needs
⬜ Letting the most senior person decide

Explanation:-
Interest-based problem-solving looks past the stated positions to the real interests behind them, often revealing solutions positional bargaining would miss.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

What is generally the most appropriate role for an Advanced Scrum Master when conflict arises between two team members?

⬜ Decide who is right and instruct the other to comply
✅ Facilitate a constructive conversation so the team members work through the conflict themselves
⬜ Report the conflict to HR immediately in all cases
⬜ Ignore it since it will resolve itself

Explanation:-
The ASM acts as a facilitator and coach who helps parties communicate and resolve issues, rather than acting as a judge or decision-maker.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

A disagreement between two teams on an ART about a shared dependency cannot be resolved despite repeated attempts at the team level. What should the Advanced Scrum Master do?

⬜ Let the disagreement continue since ASMs shouldn’t get involved in cross-team issues
✅ Escalate and facilitate resolution at the ART level, involving the Release Train Engineer if needed
⬜ Instruct one team to unilaterally give in
⬜ Wait until the next Program Increment to address it

Explanation:-
When conflict can’t be resolved at the team level, it should be escalated and facilitated at the appropriate level — often with RTE involvement — rather than left unresolved.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/scrum-master-team-coach

A team has the same disagreement resurface in every retrospective without real resolution. What technique should the Advanced Scrum Master introduce to address the recurring pattern?

⬜ Skip retrospectives until the conflict fades
✅ Use root-cause analysis techniques, such as the 5 Whys, to identify and address the underlying cause rather than the symptoms
⬜ Rotate team members every Sprint to avoid the friction
⬜ Simply document the conflict without further action

Explanation:-
Recurring conflict is often a symptom of a deeper root cause; techniques like the 5 Whys help the team get past symptoms to the actual issue.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/inspect-and-adapt

Domain 5: Improving ART Performance

What are the three main parts of the SAFe Inspect and Adapt (I&A) event?

✅ PI System Demo, Quantitative and Qualitative Measurement, and the Problem-Solving Workshop
⬜ Sprint Planning, Daily Stand-up, and Sprint Review
⬜ PI Planning, Backlog Refinement, and Retrospective
⬜ Vision, Roadmap, and Backlog

Explanation:-
The I&A event structures ART-level inspection and improvement into these three distinct parts at the end of every Program Increment.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/inspect-and-adapt

What is the primary purpose of the PI System Demo within the Inspect and Adapt event?

⬜ To plan the next Program Increment’s objectives
✅ To demonstrate the integrated, working system increment to stakeholders and gather their feedback
⬜ To calculate individual team member performance ratings
⬜ To resolve conflicts between teams

Explanation:-
The PI System Demo shows the full, integrated solution built across the ART during the PI so stakeholders can see real progress and give feedback.
Source: https://framework.scaledagile.com/inspect-and-adapt

How is the ART Predictability Measure typically calculated?

⬜ By counting the number of stories completed regardless of value
✅ By aggregating each team’s actual versus planned business value achieved for their PI Objectives
⬜ By averaging individual developer story points
⬜ By counting the number of retrospectives held

Explanation:-
The ART Predictability Measure reflects how well the ART, in aggregate, delivered against the planned business value of its PI Objectives — reliable ARTs typically score 80-100%.

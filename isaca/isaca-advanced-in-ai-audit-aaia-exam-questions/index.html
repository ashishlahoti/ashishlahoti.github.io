Free ISACA Advanced in AI Audit (AAIA) Online Practice Exam

Overview

  1. AAIA (Advanced in AI Audit) is ISACA’s advanced credential for experienced IT auditors who audit AI systems.
  2. To be eligible you need an active CISA, or one of several accepted designations (such as CIA or CPA) with an IT audit or advisory focus.
  3. The exam has 90 multiple-choice questions in 2.5 hours (150 minutes); every question has one best answer.
  4. Scores are scaled from 200 to 800, and 450 or higher passes; this practice test uses 70% as the target.
  5. The exam costs US$459 for ISACA members and US$599 for non-members, at PSI test centers or remotely proctored.
  6. The three domains are weighted 33%, 46% and 21%, so this test has 90 questions split 30 / 41 / 19.
  7. Exam content outline and AAIA page for more details.

Domain 1: AI Governance and Risk (33%)

An IT auditor is starting an AI governance review at a bank. What should the auditor confirm first?

⬜ That the organization keeps an inventory of AI and models in use, in development and recently retired
⬜ That all models are built in-house
⬜ That every model uses the same algorithm
⬜ That the AI team has the latest GPUs

Under SR 11-7, which of these counts as a model?

⬜ Any spreadsheet that stores data
⬜ Only systems that use neural networks
⬜ A quantitative method that turns input data into quantitative estimates using statistical, economic, financial or mathematical theories
⬜ Only models built by a vendor

According to SR 11-7, what are the two main sources of model risk?

⬜ Too many stakeholders and too much documentation
⬜ Fundamental errors that produce inaccurate outputs, and incorrect use or misunderstood limitations
⬜ Vendor pricing and licensing
⬜ High compute cost and slow inference

What does SR 11-7 mean by “effective challenge”?

⬜ The model developer re-checking their own work
⬜ Stakeholders voting on model approval
⬜ A penetration test of the model’s API
⬜ Critical review by objective, informed parties who can identify limitations and drive changes

A bank buys a credit-scoring model from a vendor and assumes no validation is needed. What should the auditor conclude?

⬜ Validation applies equally to vendor models, so the bank must still validate the model and understand its limitations
⬜ Vendor models are exempt from validation
⬜ The vendor’s marketing materials are enough
⬜ Only the vendor’s auditors need to review it

Which group does SR 11-7 make responsible for assessing whether the model risk management framework is effective?

⬜ The vendor
⬜ Internal audit
⬜ The marketing department
⬜ The model developers

In the NIST AI RMF, which function is cross-cutting and sets up the culture, policies and accountability for managing AI risk?

⬜ Measure
⬜ Manage
⬜ Govern
⬜ Map

Which NIST AI RMF function sets the context for an AI system’s risks and informs an initial go/no-go decision on designing or deploying it?

⬜ Measure
⬜ Manage
⬜ Govern
⬜ Map

An organization plans responses to, recovery from and communication about AI incidents. Which AI RMF function does this belong to?

⬜ Measure
⬜ Manage
⬜ Map
⬜ Govern

Which trustworthy AI characteristic does the NIST AI RMF treat as the base that the others build on?

⬜ Valid and reliable
⬜ Explainable and interpretable
⬜ Fair with harmful bias managed
⬜ Privacy-enhanced

A business owner says the AI system is transparent, so it must also be fair and secure. How should the auditor respond?

⬜ Accept it if the vendor certifies the system
⬜ Agree, as long as the code is open source
⬜ Agree, because transparency implies the other characteristics
⬜ Transparency doesn’t guarantee that a system is accurate, private, secure or fair; each characteristic needs its own evidence

A team improves a model’s accuracy by switching to a far more complex model. Which trade-off does the AI RMF warn about?

⬜ Complex models are always more secure
⬜ Higher accuracy always improves fairness
⬜ Optimizing for predictive accuracy can reduce interpretability
⬜ Accuracy and privacy never conflict

What is the status of the NIST AI RMF 1.0?

⬜ An EU law
⬜ It’s voluntary guidance released in January 2023, with a Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1) released in July 2024
⬜ An ISO certification scheme
⬜ A binding US regulation with fines

Under the EU AI Act, which practice falls in the unacceptable-risk (prohibited) category?

⬜ Social scoring that ranks people in ways that lead to unfavourable treatment
⬜ AI-assisted credit scoring
⬜ A spam filter
⬜ A customer service chatbot

Under the EU AI Act, who carries most obligations for high-risk AI systems?

⬜ End users only
⬜ Deployers only
⬜ No one, until an incident occurs
⬜ Providers (developers) of the high-risk systems, including providers outside the EU whose output is used in the EU

An EU company deploys a customer chatbot. Which AI Act obligation mainly applies?

⬜ Registration as a social-scoring system
⬜ No obligations of any kind
⬜ Transparency, so that people know they are interacting with AI
⬜ A full conformity assessment as a prohibited system

An insurer plans to use AI to profile applicants and automatically decide premiums. Under GDPR, what must it do before processing?

⬜ Carry out a data protection impact assessment (DPIA), because this is systematic, extensive automated evaluation with significant effects
⬜ Only get approval from the IT department
⬜ Nothing, if the model is accurate
⬜ Only notify customers after the first decision

What must a GDPR DPIA contain at a minimum?

⬜ Only the vendor contract
⬜ A description of the processing and its purposes, an assessment of necessity and proportionality, an assessment of the risks to individuals, and the measures to address those risks
⬜ Only a list of the data fields
⬜ Only the model’s accuracy metrics

A lender’s AI automatically rejects loan applications with no human involvement. Which GDPR provision should the auditor check compliance against?

⬜ No GDPR provision applies to AI
⬜ Recital 26 only
⬜ Article 22, the right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects, with safeguards such as human intervention
⬜ Article 35 only

An AI team says its training data is pseudonymized, so privacy controls aren’t needed. What should the auditor conclude?

⬜ Only names count as personal data
⬜ GDPR never applies to training data
⬜ Pseudonymization makes data anonymous
⬜ Pseudonymized data is still personal data under GDPR, so privacy controls still apply

Which of these is listed as a generative AI risk in NIST AI 600-1?

⬜ Slow GPUs
⬜ Excessive model accuracy
⬜ Confabulation, meaning confidently stated but false content
⬜ Too many model versions

An organization builds a generative AI product on third-party models, datasets and plugins. Which NIST AI 600-1 risk applies most directly?

⬜ Environmental impacts only
⬜ Obscene content only
⬜ CBRN information only
⬜ Value chain and component integration

Which document best tells auditors a model’s intended use and its performance across demographic groups?

⬜ A network diagram
⬜ A model card
⬜ The project Gantt chart
⬜ The vendor invoice

A model is being used in a context its documentation says it wasn’t designed for. What’s the primary risk?

⬜ Misuse outside the intended context, where its limitations and assumptions may not hold
⬜ Licensing fees
⬜ Slower training
⬜ Higher hosting costs

Which governance control most directly ensures that each AI risk has a clear owner?

⬜ Documented accountability structures that assign ownership of AI risks, controls and decisions
⬜ Publishing a press release on AI ethics
⬜ Buying cyber insurance
⬜ Increasing the AI team’s budget

An organization’s AI policy exists, but no one checks whether teams follow it. What’s the auditor’s main concern?

⬜ Nothing, as having a policy is enough
⬜ The policy uses the wrong font
⬜ The policy is too short
⬜ There’s no evidence the policy is operating; the auditor should test compliance rather than rely on the policy document

Which AI RMF statement about bias is accurate?

⬜ Bias only matters in image models
⬜ Mitigating harmful bias doesn’t by itself make a system fair
⬜ Fairness is the same as accuracy
⬜ Removing protected attributes guarantees fairness

An auditor finds AI systems being deployed with no prior risk assessment. Which recommendation best addresses this?

⬜ Ban all AI immediately
⬜ Assess risk only after an incident
⬜ Require an AI risk assessment (context, impacts and risks) before design or deployment decisions, as part of governance
⬜ Let each developer decide informally

A bank’s individual models each look acceptable, but many of them share the same data source and assumptions. What does SR 11-7 expect?

⬜ Stop using shared data entirely
⬜ Assess each model alone and ignore interactions
⬜ Leave it to the vendor
⬜ Manage model risk in aggregate as well as model by model, since shared inputs and assumptions can concentrate risk

High-risk AI systems under the EU AI Act face which kind of requirements?

⬜ Risk management, data governance, technical documentation, logging, human oversight and registration in an EU database
⬜ None, if the system is accurate
⬜ Only an annual financial audit
⬜ Only a privacy notice

Domain 2: AI Operations (46%)

An auditor needs to confirm that a model’s training data came from approved sources and see how it was transformed. Which evidence is most useful?

⬜ The cloud invoice
⬜ Data lineage records showing origins, transformations and movement of the data
⬜ An interview with the data scientist only
⬜ The model’s accuracy dashboard

Which role manages a data domain day to day and communicates governance policies to data producers and consumers?

⬜ The external auditor
⬜ The model end user
⬜ The data steward
⬜ The data owner

A fraud model’s performance slowly declines because customer behavior has changed. What is this called, and what control addresses it?

⬜ Data poisoning; deleting the model
⬜ Prompt injection; input filtering
⬜ Overfitting; more training epochs
⬜ Model drift; continuous monitoring with thresholds and retraining

Production input data has shifted in distribution, but the relationship between inputs and outcomes hasn’t changed. What type of drift is this?

⬜ Model extraction
⬜ Data drift (covariate shift)
⬜ Concept drift
⬜ Label leakage

Which statistical techniques does IBM cite for detecting drift by comparing training data with live data?

⬜ The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, Wasserstein distance and the population stability index (PSI)
⬜ Gantt charts
⬜ Net present value
⬜ Story points

An upstream system silently changes its currency field from USD to EUR, and model outputs degrade. What should the auditor recommend?

⬜ Turn off monitoring
⬜ Increase the model’s size
⬜ Input data validation and change management over upstream data pipelines feeding the model
⬜ Retrain on random data

What is the most important control when promoting a new model version to production?

⬜ Overwriting the old model with no record
⬜ Skipping tests to meet deadlines
⬜ Deploying directly from a developer’s laptop
⬜ Change management with versioning of data, models and code, testing and approval, and the ability to roll back

Why does MLOps emphasize reproducibility?

⬜ To remove the need for monitoring
⬜ To make models run faster
⬜ So experiments and deployments can be repeated, which supports debugging, comparison and audit
⬜ To avoid documentation

A deployed credit model hasn’t been retrained in three years, although market conditions have changed. Which SR 11-7 validation element is most likely missing?

⬜ Conceptual soundness at design time only
⬜ Ongoing monitoring that checks whether changes in products or markets require the model to be adjusted
⬜ The vendor’s sales demo
⬜ The model’s color scheme

Which SR 11-7 validation element compares model outputs with actual outcomes, for example through back-testing?

⬜ Outcomes analysis
⬜ Conceptual soundness
⬜ Effective challenge
⬜ Model inventory

Which SR 11-7 validation element reviews a model’s design, construction, documentation and empirical support?

⬜ Ongoing monitoring
⬜ Outcomes analysis
⬜ Evaluation of conceptual soundness
⬜ Back-testing

How often does SR 11-7 expect models to be reviewed after deployment?

⬜ Every five years
⬜ Validation continues after deployment, with reviews at least annually
⬜ Only when regulators ask
⬜ Only once, before go-live

Who should validate a model, according to SR 11-7?

⬜ The business line that profits from it
⬜ The developer who built it
⬜ The vendor’s sales team
⬜ Staff independent of the model’s development and use

Under the EU AI Act, what must people overseeing a high-risk AI system be able to do?

⬜ Understand its capabilities and limits, be aware of automation bias, and override, reverse or stop it
⬜ Nothing, as oversight is optional
⬜ Only read its marketing brochure
⬜ Only view monthly reports

Reviewers approve almost every AI recommendation without checking it. Which risk does the AI Act specifically ask overseers to be aware of?

⬜ Model extraction
⬜ Confirmation of model accuracy
⬜ Automation bias, meaning over-reliance on the system’s outputs
⬜ Data minimization

A high-risk AI system has no way for an operator to halt it during a malfunction. Which requirement is missing?

⬜ A model card
⬜ A stop button or similar procedure that lets humans intervene or halt the system
⬜ A data lineage tool
⬜ A DPIA

An auditor reviews a fraud model where missing fraud is very costly. Which metric should management’s testing emphasize?

⬜ Precision only
⬜ Accuracy only
⬜ Training time
⬜ Recall, which is the share of actual fraud cases the model catches

A model reports 99% accuracy on a dataset where 1% of cases are positive. What should the auditor do?

⬜ Challenge the metric, because a model that always predicts the majority class also scores 99%; ask for precision and recall
⬜ Ignore metrics entirely
⬜ Ask only for training time
⬜ Accept the result as strong evidence

Test results show very low error on training data and much higher error on held-out test data. What does this indicate for the auditor?

⬜ Data drift in production
⬜ Excellent generalization
⬜ Underfitting
⬜ Overfitting, so the model may not generalize to production data

Before releasing a generative AI system, what testing does NIST AI 600-1 recommend?

⬜ Pre-deployment evaluation of capabilities and safety measures, including AI red-teaming, with results shared with release approvers
⬜ Only load testing
⬜ No testing, since generative AI can’t be tested
⬜ Only a user satisfaction survey after release

A chatbot reads customer emails, and one email contains hidden instructions that make the bot leak data. What threat is this?

⬜ Data drift
⬜ Model inversion
⬜ Indirect prompt injection
⬜ Overfitting

Which controls does OWASP recommend to reduce the impact of prompt injection?

⬜ Disable all logging
⬜ Constrain model behavior, filter inputs and outputs, enforce least privilege, require human approval for high-risk actions, and mark untrusted content
⬜ Give the model admin access
⬜ Rely only on the system prompt

An attacker corrupts the fine-tuning data so a model behaves normally until a trigger phrase appears. What is this?

⬜ Model drift
⬜ A data poisoning backdoor (a “sleeper agent”)
⬜ Prompt injection
⬜ Membership inference

Which control best helps detect manipulation of training datasets?

⬜ Using larger GPUs
⬜ Raising the model’s temperature
⬜ Removing all logging
⬜ Data version control and tracking data origins and transformations (for example, an ML-BOM)

Attackers add tiny changes to images at inference so a classifier misreads them. What type of attack is this, and what is a defense?

⬜ Prompt injection; a bigger context window
⬜ Model extraction; adding GPUs
⬜ An evasion attack; adversarial training improves robustness
⬜ Data poisoning; deleting the model

An attacker queries a public model API many times to build a copy of it. What threat is this?

⬜ Model extraction
⬜ Data drift
⬜ Evasion
⬜ Concept drift

Which attack reveals whether a specific person’s record was in the training data?

⬜ Evasion
⬜ Model extraction
⬜ Data drift
⬜ Membership inference

An auditor wants a structured catalog of adversary tactics and techniques against AI systems to scope threat testing. Which resource fits?

⬜ PRINCE2
⬜ MITRE ATLAS
⬜ COBIT
⬜ ITIL

Which practice does NIST AI 600-1 recommend for AI incident handling?

⬜ Waiting for regulators to discover incidents
⬜ Deleting the model after any incident
⬜ Defined criteria for incident reporting and communication, and after-action reviews of incident response and disclosure
⬜ Keeping all incidents confidential from affected users

After an AI incident, the team restores service but doesn’t analyze the cause. What’s the auditor’s concern?

⬜ Without root-cause analysis and an after-action review, the incident may recur and lessons aren’t learned
⬜ No concern, since service is restored
⬜ The model should be renamed
⬜ The incident should be hidden

An LLM answers policy questions with invented citations. Which control best reduces this risk?

⬜ Raise the temperature
⬜ Ground answers in curated sources with RAG and require human review for high-stakes outputs
⬜ Remove the system prompt
⬜ Train on more unvetted web data

A deep learning loan model can’t explain individual decisions, and regulators require explanations. Which approach helps?

⬜ Removing the test set
⬜ Data augmentation
⬜ Bigger GPUs
⬜ Explainable AI techniques such as LIME that explain individual predictions

Which is the best evidence that a model was approved before deployment?

⬜ A verbal confirmation from the developer
⬜ An email saying “looks fine” with no version
⬜ Documented approval records tied to a specific model version in a controlled registry or change system
⬜ A screenshot with no date

Which lifecycle stage is most often overlooked in AI governance but still needs controls?

⬜ Decommissioning, including retiring models, revoking access and handling their data
⬜ Training
⬜ Testing
⬜ Design

Which access control matters most for an AI training environment holding sensitive data?

⬜ Open access for all employees
⬜ Least-privilege access with periodic reviews of who can access the data and models
⬜ No access logging

Before using customer data to train a model, what should the organization evaluate?

⬜ Only the column names
⬜ Only the file size

⬜ Only GPU utilization
⬜ Only the number of models built
⬜ Only the developers’ working hours

Change management for AI differs from traditional software mainly because:

⬜ Model behavior can change through new data or retraining even when the code doesn’t change
⬜ AI code can’t be versioned
⬜ AI systems don’t have users
⬜ AI never needs testing

Which vendor-risk control is most important when using third-party AI models?

⬜ Skipping validation because the vendor is large
⬜ Validating the vendor model against the organization’s own use and data, and understanding its limitations
⬜ Trusting the vendor’s marketing claims
⬜ Assuming the vendor carries all risk

⬜ Slower inference only
⬜ Lower licensing costs

⬜ Capacity planning
⬜ Data labeling

Domain 3: AI Auditing Tools and Techniques (21%)

What two qualities must audit evidence have?

⬜ It must be sufficient (enough of it) and appropriate (relevant and reliable)
⬜ It must come from management and be signed
⬜ It must be digital and recent
⬜ It must be quantitative and automated

An auditor asks the data science lead whether bias testing was done and gets a confident “yes”. Is this enough?

⬜ Yes, if the answer is recorded in an email
⬜ Yes, if two people agree
⬜ Yes, because the lead is senior
⬜ No; inquiry by itself doesn’t provide sufficient evidence, so the auditor should inspect test results or reperform the tests

Which evidence about a model’s performance is generally the most reliable?

⬜ A verbal description from a user
⬜ A vendor brochure
⬜ Results the auditor obtains directly by reperforming the evaluation on an independent holdout dataset
⬜ A summary slide from the model owner

An auditor recalculates a model’s reported precision from its logged predictions and outcomes. Which procedure is this?

⬜ Observation
⬜ Recalculation (or reperformance)
⬜ Confirmation
⬜ Inquiry

Why is observing a model retraining run limited as audit evidence?

⬜ Observation only covers the moment it takes place and may not show how the process works at other times
⬜ Observation is never allowed
⬜ Observation replaces documentation
⬜ Observation is always conclusive

Evidence from two sources about a model’s validation conflicts. What should the auditor do?

⬜ Ignore both sources
⬜ Investigate the inconsistency and assess its effect on the audit before concluding
⬜ Ask management which one to use and accept the answer
⬜ Pick the more favorable source

An auditor tests 60 of 2,000 AI decision records. What is this procedure called?

⬜ Full population testing
⬜ Observation
⬜ Audit sampling, which applies a procedure to less than 100% of the items in a population

How should sample items be selected so conclusions can be extended to the population?

⬜ By choosing the easiest records to review
⬜ By only choosing the most recent day’s records
⬜ By letting management pick the items
⬜ So the sample can be expected to be representative, for example by random or systematic selection with a random start

Which risk comes from testing a sample rather than the whole population?

⬜ Sampling risk
⬜ Model risk
⬜ Inherent risk
⬜ Nonsampling risk

An auditor uses the wrong test to evaluate model fairness. What kind of risk is this?

⬜ Nonsampling risk, which is reduced through planning, supervision and quality control
⬜ Sampling risk
⬜ No risk
⬜ Detection risk eliminated by larger samples

In a test of an AI control, which sampling error affects audit effectiveness by making the control look more reliable than it is?

⬜ Nonsampling efficiency loss
⬜ Assessing control risk too low
⬜ Assessing control risk too high
⬜ Incorrect rejection

With AI-enabled analytics, an auditor can test all 2,000 automated decisions instead of a sample. What does this eliminate?

⬜ Nonsampling risk
⬜ All audit risk of any kind
⬜ The need to plan the audit
⬜ Sampling risk for that test, since 100% of the population is examined

Which framework provides auditors with key practices and audit procedures for AI across governance, data, performance and monitoring?

⬜ PMBOK
⬜ PRINCE2
⬜ The GAO AI Accountability Framework (GAO-21-519SP)
⬜ ITIL 4

When planning an AI audit, what should the auditor do first?

⬜ Start testing random models immediately
⬜ Understand the AI system’s purpose, context and risks to scope the audit on the highest-risk areas
⬜ Ask developers to self-certify
⬜ Write the final report

How does assessed risk affect the amount of evidence the auditor needs?

⬜ Higher risk requires more evidence, while better-quality evidence needs less corroboration
⬜ The amount is always the same
⬜ More weak evidence makes up for poor quality
⬜ Higher risk requires less evidence

An auditor wants to use a generative AI tool to summarize hundreds of model documents during fieldwork. What’s the key consideration?

⬜ Upload confidential data to any public tool
⬜ Accept its summaries without review
⬜ Assess the tool’s risks, such as confabulation and data privacy, and verify its outputs before relying on them as evidence
⬜ Avoid documenting how the tool was used

Which statement about the data an auditor analyzes is correct?

⬜ Analytics replaces the need for evidence
⬜ System data is always reliable
⬜ Data quality only matters for external data
⬜ The auditor must assess the reliability of system-generated data, which depends on controls over that data

What should an AI audit report communicate to management?

⬜ Only technical details of the algorithms
⬜ The findings with their risks and root causes, recommendations, and management’s agreed actions with owners and dates
⬜ Only a pass or fail score
⬜ Only the auditor’s working hours

An auditor finds that no AI system has an assigned risk owner. How should this be reported?

⬜ As no finding, since the models work
⬜ As an IT hardware issue
⬜ As a governance finding, recommending that ownership of AI risks, controls and decisions be assigned and documented
⬜ As a developer performance issue

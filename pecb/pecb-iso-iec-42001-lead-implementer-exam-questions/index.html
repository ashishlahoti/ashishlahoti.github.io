Overview

  1. The PECB Certified ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer credential shows you can plan, implement, monitor and continually improve an artificial intelligence management system (AIMS) based on ISO/IEC 42001:2023, and prepare an organization for its certification audit.
  2. The exam has 80 multiple-choice questions, each with three options, in 3 hours. Some questions stand alone; others come in sets of five built around a scenario. It’s open book: a hard copy of ISO/IEC 42001, the course materials and your notes are allowed.
  3. Passing score is 70%. After passing, the credential level (Provisional Implementer, Implementer, Lead Implementer or Senior Lead Implementer) depends on your work and AIMS project experience.
  4. The six competency domains carry 17, 5, 20, 18, 10 and 10 questions, so this test has the same 80 questions split 17 / 5 / 20 / 18 / 10 / 10.
  5. Candidate handbook and course brochure for more details.

Domain 1: Fundamental principles and concepts of an AI management system (17 questions (21.25%))

A bank asks what ISO/IEC 42001 actually specifies. Which description is correct?

⬜ Requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining and continually improving an AI management system
⬜ A list of AI practices that are banned for organizations operating in the EU
⬜ Technical test methods for measuring the accuracy, robustness and bias of machine learning models before deployment

A ten-person startup that only uses a third-party AI chatbot asks whether ISO/IEC 42001 applies to organizations like it. What’s the answer?

⬜ Yes; it applies to organizations of any size or type that supply or use AI systems
⬜ No; it applies only to organizations that develop and train their own AI models in-house
⬜ No; it applies only to organizations with more than 250 employees

ISO/IEC 22989 defines an AI system as an engineered system that does what?

⬜ Generates outputs such as content, forecasts, recommendations or decisions for a given set of human-defined objectives
⬜ Runs only on specialized hardware such as GPUs inside a data centre
⬜ Copies human consciousness and emotions so that it can act entirely on its own, without any objectives that people define for it

A customer believes an ISO/IEC 42001 certificate proves the supplier complies with the EU AI Act. Is that right?

⬜ No; an ISO/IEC 42001 certificate doesn’t establish conformity with the EU AI Act
⬜ Yes, but only for AI systems classified as high-risk under the Act
⬜ Yes; the EU AI Act accepts ISO/IEC 42001 certification as full proof of compliance

An organization already has an ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS and an ISO 9001 QMS. Which part of ISO/IEC 42001 gives informative guidance on using the AIMS alongside other management system standards?

⬜ Annex A
⬜ Annex D
⬜ Clause 2

Which event is regarded as the birth of artificial intelligence as a field of research?

⬜ The 1956 Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence
⬜ IBM’s Deep Blue computer defeating world chess champion Garry Kasparov in 1997
⬜ The publication of ISO/IEC 22989 terminology in 2022

In 1950, Alan Turing proposed a test. What was it designed to assess?

⬜ The speed at which a computer can solve mathematical equations compared with a human
⬜ A machine’s ability to show intelligent behaviour indistinguishable from a human’s
⬜ Whether a neural network has enough layers to count as deep learning

What was the ‘First AI Winter’ (conventionally 1966–1974)?

⬜ A period when AI research moved to cold climates to cool its computers
⬜ A period of reduced funding and progress after AI failed to meet early hype
⬜ The years when the EU first banned social scoring systems

An AI system recognizes faces in photos extremely well but can’t do anything else. How is this type of AI usually described?

⬜ Artificial superintelligence
⬜ Narrow AI (weak AI, or artificial narrow intelligence)
⬜ Artificial general intelligence

An email platform learns to filter spam by spotting patterns in messages users have marked as spam, without rules written for each case. What is this an example of?

⬜ Robotic process automation
⬜ A rule-based expert system
⬜ Machine learning

A team trains a model with images that are each labelled ‘defect’ or ’no defect’. Which learning method is this?

⬜ Unsupervised learning
⬜ Reinforcement learning
⬜ Supervised learning

A marketing team groups customers into segments from unlabelled purchase data, without telling the model what the groups should be. Which learning method fits?

⬜ Reinforcement learning
⬜ Unsupervised learning
⬜ Supervised learning

A warehouse robot improves its route choices through trial and error, guided by successful outcomes rather than labelled examples. Which learning method is this?

⬜ Reinforcement learning
⬜ Supervised learning
⬜ Unsupervised learning

How does deep learning relate to machine learning?

⬜ It’s a subset of machine learning that trains artificial neural networks with many layers
⬜ It’s a type of rule-based system that replaced machine learning
⬜ It’s a separate field of AI that doesn’t need training data, because experts write all its rules by hand

A city council wants an AI system that scores residents on their social behaviour to decide access to public services. How does the EU AI Act classify this?

⬜ Unacceptable risk, so it’s prohibited
⬜ High risk, allowed with strict obligations
⬜ Transparency risk, allowed with disclosure

An HR team uses an AI tool to sort CVs for recruitment. Under the EU AI Act’s risk-based approach, which category does this use case fall into?

⬜ High risk
⬜ Unacceptable risk
⬜ Minimal risk

A retailer adds a customer-service chatbot to its website. What does the EU AI Act require for this transparency-risk system?

⬜ The chatbot must pass a conformity assessment before launch
⬜ Users must be told they’re interacting with a machine
⬜ Nothing, because chatbots are minimal-risk systems

Domain 2: Artificial intelligence management system (5 questions (6.25%))

An implementer asks which parts of ISO/IEC 42001 an organization must meet to claim conformity. What’s correct?

⬜ Only the clauses the organization chooses to apply
⬜ Only Annex C objectives and risk sources
⬜ All ‘shall’ requirements in Clauses 4 to 10

How many reference controls does Annex A of ISO/IEC 42001 contain, and how are they organized?

⬜ 38 controls in nine areas, A.2 to A.10
⬜ 93 controls in four themes
⬜ 114 controls in 14 domains

What’s the role of Annex B in ISO/IEC 42001?

⬜ Implementation guidance (‘should’) for each Annex A control
⬜ A list of AI practices banned by regulators, with examples for each sector
⬜ A template for the certification audit report

An organization is brainstorming AI objectives and AI risk sources for its assessment. Which annex lists potential examples?

⬜ Annex C (informative)
⬜ Annex A (normative)
⬜ Annex D (informative)

Which ISO/IEC 42001 clause covers internal audit and management review?

⬜ Clause 10, Improvement
⬜ Clause 8, Operation
⬜ Clause 9, Performance evaluation

Domain 3: Planning of an AIMS implementation (20 questions (25%))

While analysing its context under Clause 4.1, what must an organization determine about climate change?

⬜ Its carbon footprint for every AI model it trains
⬜ Whether climate change is a relevant issue for its AIMS
⬜ Nothing; climate change is out of scope

TechCo builds an AI hiring tool and also uses a third-party AI translation service. What must it do for each AI system under Clause 4.1?

⬜ Consider its intended purpose and determine TechCo’s role for it
⬜ Ask the certification body to assign the roles
⬜ Assume the same role for every system to keep the AIMS simple

In the roles named in ISO/IEC 42001, which role fits an organization that develops and deploys its own AI models?

⬜ AI producer

⬜ Relevant authority

⬜ AI partner
⬜ AI subject

⬜ Only the requirements of its shareholders

⬜ Only when the certification body requests it

⬜ Documented, communicated internally, and available to interested parties as appropriate

Before assessing AI risks, what does Clause 6.1.1 require the organization to establish and maintain?

⬜ AI risk criteria for risk acceptance, assessment, treatment and impact assessment
⬜ A list of approved AI programming languages, frameworks and model types for every development team
⬜ A budget for buying AI insurance policies

An AI risk assessment process must produce what kind of results under Clause 6.1.2?

⬜ Results that always show low risk, so the AI systems can launch without treatment
⬜ Consistent, valid and comparable results
⬜ Results approved by the AI vendor

During risk treatment, the team picks its controls. Why must it compare them with Annex A?

⬜ To make sure every Annex A control is adopted
⬜ To replace all its own controls with Annex A ones
⬜ To check that no necessary controls have been omitted

A consultant says ISO/IEC 42001 forces organizations to implement all Annex A controls. Is that right?

⬜ No; the organization must consider them and include only those its risk treatment needs, justifying exclusions
⬜ Yes, but only for organizations in the AI producer role
⬜ Yes; certification requires every Annex A control to be implemented, although Annex B guidance stays optional

What must the Statement of Applicability contain for each Annex A control?

⬜ A justification for its inclusion or exclusion
⬜ The name of the employee who wrote the control
⬜ The cost of implementing the control

An ISO/IEC 27001 expert expects the AIMS Statement of Applicability to show whether each control is implemented. What does ISO/IEC 42001 require?

⬜ Implementation status is required with a percentage for each control
⬜ Implementation status replaces the justification for exclusions
⬜ Implementation status isn’t a required item, unlike in ISO/IEC 27001

After treatment, some AI risk remains. Who approves the risk treatment plan and accepts the residual risk?

⬜ The certification body
⬜ The data science team
⬜ Designated management

HealthCo plans an AI triage tool. Its AI system impact assessment must consider effects on whom?

⬜ Only the organization’s shareholders and the revenue the AI tool is expected to generate
⬜ Individuals, groups of individuals and societies
⬜ Only the developers who built the system

HealthCo’s impact assessment for the triage tool must look beyond intended use. What else must it consider?

⬜ The marketing budget for the launch
⬜ Reasonably foreseeable misuse
⬜ The developers’ job satisfaction

How should the results of AI system impact assessments be used in the AIMS?

⬜ They’re kept only for the certification audit
⬜ They feed into the AI risk assessment
⬜ They replace the AI risk assessment

The team sets AI objectives under Clause 6.2. Which statement is correct?

⬜ They must be set once and never updated
⬜ They must be measurable if practicable, with planned actions, resources, owners and completion dates
⬜ They must be kept secret from staff and competitors, and set once for the whole certification cycle

The organization decides its AIMS needs significant changes. How does Clause 6.3 say these must be made?

⬜ In a planned manner
⬜ Immediately, without planning
⬜ Only after recertification

ShopCo wants controls for assessing how its recommendation engine affects society, not just individual customers. Which Annex A control addresses this?

⬜ A.7.6 Data preparation
⬜ A.5.5 Assessing societal impacts of AI systems
⬜ A.2.4 Review of the AI policy

Auditors ask how the organization can reconstruct what its AI system did during an incident. Which Annex A control is most relevant?

⬜ A.3.2 AI roles and responsibilities
⬜ A.6.2.8 AI system recording of event logs
⬜ A.10.4 Customers

A model was trained on data bought from several brokers, and nobody can say where each record came from. Which Annex A control addresses this gap?

⬜ A.7.5 Data provenance
⬜ A.8.3 External reporting
⬜ A.4.5 System and computing resources

Training data for a credit model contains many duplicates and missing values. Which Annex A control is directly concerned?

⬜ A.9.4 Intended use of the AI system

⬜ A.7.4 Quality of data for AI systems

An AI system produced harmful outputs for customers. Which Annex A control requires a way to tell users about such incidents?

⬜ A.4.4 Tooling resources
⬜ A.6.2.5 AI system deployment
⬜ A.8.4 Communication of incidents

Employees should be able to raise concerns about the organization’s AI systems through a defined process. Which Annex A control covers this?

⬜ A.6.2.7 AI system technical documentation
⬜ A.3.3 Reporting of concerns
⬜ A.7.3 Acquisition of data

A team uses a forecasting model for a purpose it was never designed for. Which Annex A control is most relevant?

⬜ A.4.6 Human resources
⬜ A.9.4 Intended use of the AI system
⬜ A.5.3 Documentation of AI system impact assessments

An organization buys an AI component from a vendor and integrates it into its product. Which Annex A area covers responsibilities with the vendor?

⬜ A.2 Policies related to AI
⬜ A.10 Third-party and customer relationships
⬜ A.6 AI system life cycle

Which Annex A area requires documenting the data, tooling, computing and human resources AI systems need?

⬜ A.5 Assessing impacts of AI systems
⬜ A.4 Resources for AI systems
⬜ A.8 Information for interested parties

The new AI policy conflicts with the existing data protection policy. Which Annex A control requires checking that they fit together?

⬜ A.6.2.4 AI system verification and validation
⬜ A.10.3 Suppliers
⬜ A.2.3 Alignment with other organizational policies

Data scientists whose work affects AI performance need the right skills. What does Clause 7.2 require as evidence?

⬜ A university degree in AI for every employee
⬜ Nothing, as competence is assumed
⬜ Documented information as evidence of competence

An awareness campaign is being designed. What must people doing work under the organization’s control be aware of under Clause 7.3?

⬜ The AI policy, their contribution to AIMS effectiveness, and the implications of nonconformity
⬜ The salaries, bonuses and performance ratings of every person who works on the AIMS project team
⬜ The certification body’s pricing

The project lead is writing a communication plan. What does Clause 7.4 require the organization to determine for AIMS communications?

⬜ What, when, with whom and how to communicate
⬜ Only the email templates for IT outages
⬜ Only the press release for the certification

What documented information must the AIMS include under Clause 7.5.1?

⬜ A fixed set of exactly 20 mandatory documents
⬜ Only the documents the certification body asks for, since it decides what the AIMS must document
⬜ What the standard requires plus what the organization finds necessary for effectiveness

The team relies on an external AI regulation guide to plan the AIMS. How should this external document be handled?

⬜ Ignored, because the AIMS controls only the documented information the organization writes itself
⬜ Copied into the AI policy word for word
⬜ Identified and controlled as documented information of external origin

Model labelling is outsourced to a vendor. What does Clause 8.1 require?

⬜ The vendor must hold ISO/IEC 42001 certification
⬜ The outsourced process must be controlled
⬜ Outsourced work is outside the AIMS

When must the organization perform AI risk assessments under Clause 8.2?

⬜ Only once, before the initial certification audit, then never again unless the auditor requests it
⬜ At planned intervals and when significant changes are proposed or occur
⬜ Only when a customer complains

A risk treatment option turns out to be ineffective. What does Clause 8.3 require?

⬜ Accept the risk automatically without review
⬜ Re-examine and revalidate it, then update the risk treatment plan
⬜ Keep it unchanged until the next recertification audit, since plans can’t change mid-cycle

Domain 5: Monitoring and measurement of an AIMS (10 questions (12.5%))

Before measuring AIMS performance, what must the organization determine under Clause 9.1?

⬜ What to monitor and measure, the methods, and when to monitor and analyse
⬜ Which competitors to benchmark against each month and which AI vendors to replace
⬜ The certification body’s audit schedule

What’s the purpose of internal audits under Clause 9.2.1?

⬜ To replace the external certification audit entirely, so that the certification body can rely on the internal results
⬜ To check the AIMS conforms to the organization’s and the standard’s requirements and is effectively implemented
⬜ To evaluate employees’ individual performance for bonuses

The lead data scientist offers to audit the model development process she designed. What does Clause 9.2.2 require?

⬜ The process owner always audits their own process, because she knows it better than anyone
⬜ Audits are done only by the certification body
⬜ Auditors are selected to ensure objectivity and impartiality

When planning the internal audit programme, what must the organization take into account?

⬜ Only the processes that passed previous audits, so effort goes into keeping them compliant
⬜ The importance of the processes and the results of previous audits
⬜ Only the processes managed by top management

Who must review the AIMS at planned intervals, and for what?

⬜ Top management, for continuing suitability, adequacy and effectiveness
⬜ The certification body, for pricing changes
⬜ The internal auditor, for spelling and formatting errors in AIMS documents and records

Which item is a required input to management review under Clause 9.3.2?

⬜ Trends in nonconformities, corrective actions, monitoring results and audit results
⬜ The personal opinions of each employee about the AI policy, collected through an anonymous yearly survey
⬜ Competitors’ AI product roadmaps

What must management review outputs include under Clause 9.3.3?

⬜ A new AI vendor for every system
⬜ Approval of next year’s salary budget and bonuses for the people who run the AIMS processes
⬜ Decisions on improvement opportunities and any needed changes to the AIMS

An auditor finds the organization has no AI risk assessment process at all. How is a nonconformity that affects the management system’s capability to achieve intended results classified?

⬜ Opportunity for improvement
⬜ Major nonconformity
⬜ Minor nonconformity

One AI policy review was signed off a week late, but the process works and is followed. How is a nonconformity that doesn’t affect the capability to achieve intended results classified?

⬜ Not a finding, since the review happened
⬜ Minor nonconformity
⬜ Major nonconformity

An auditor finds several minor nonconformities against the same requirement. How could they be treated?

⬜ Together they could show a systemic failure and be a major nonconformity
⬜ They can never be more than minor, however many there are
⬜ They cancel each other out

Domain 6: Continual improvement and preparation for an AIMS certification audit (10 questions (12.5%))

A nonconformity is found in the model approval process. What must the organization do first under Clause 10.2?

⬜ Wait for the next management review, which decides whether the nonconformity needs any action
⬜ React to it: control and correct it and deal with the consequences
⬜ Ignore it if no customer noticed

After correcting a nonconformity, how does the organization decide whether corrective action is needed to stop it recurring?

⬜ Review it, determine its causes, and check whether similar nonconformities exist or could occur
⬜ Assume it won’t happen again
⬜ Ask the certification body to decide at its next surveillance audit whether any action is needed

Corrective action has been implemented. What must happen next under Clause 10.2?

⬜ Close it without any further check
⬜ Review the effectiveness of the corrective action
⬜ Delete the nonconformity record

A team keeps asking ‘why?’ about a nonconformity until it reaches the underlying process failure. Which root cause analysis technique is this?

⬜ A SWOT analysis
⬜ The 5 Whys
⬜ A Gantt chart

What does Clause 10.1 require the organization to continually improve?

⬜ The accuracy of every AI model to 100%
⬜ The suitability, adequacy and effectiveness of the AIMS
⬜ The number of AI systems in use each year, as proof the AIMS supports innovation

How long is a typical management system certification cycle, and how often are surveillance audits held?

⬜ Five years, with one surveillance audit
⬜ One year, with no surveillance audits
⬜ Three years, with surveillance audits every year

How is the initial certification audit structured?

⬜ As a document review only
⬜ In two stages, Stage 1 and Stage 2
⬜ As a single unannounced visit

What should the organization check when selecting a certification body for ISO/IEC 42001?

⬜ That it also helped design and implement the AIMS, so its auditors already know the system well
⬜ That it’s accredited and its accreditation covers ISO/IEC 42001
⬜ That it offers the lowest price

Which standard sets additional requirements for bodies that audit and certify AI management systems?

⬜ ISO/IEC 42006, which builds on ISO/IEC 17021-1
⬜ ISO 9001, which covers quality management
⬜ ISO/IEC 22989, which defines AI terms

During certification, who decides which Annex A controls apply to the organization?

⬜ The certification body, which selects the applicable controls in its audit plan before Stage 1
⬜ The accreditation body, through a standard list
⬜ The organization, through risk treatment and its Statement of Applicability

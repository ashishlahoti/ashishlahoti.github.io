Free PECB Certified ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Auditor Online Practice Exam

Overview

  1. The PECB Certified ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Auditor credential shows you can plan, conduct, report on and follow up an information security management system (ISMS) audit based on ISO/IEC 27001:2022, using ISO 19011 and ISO/IEC 17021-1 practices.
  2. The exam has 80 multiple-choice questions, each with three options, in 3 hours. Some questions stand alone; others come in sets of five built around a scenario. It’s open book: a hard copy of ISO/IEC 27001, the course materials and your notes are allowed.
  3. Passing score is 70%. After passing, the credential level (Provisional Auditor, Auditor, Lead Auditor or Senior Lead Auditor) depends on your work and audit experience.
  4. The seven competency domains carry 13, 8, 14, 12, 18, 7 and 8 questions, so this test has the same 80 questions split 13 / 8 / 14 / 12 / 18 / 7 / 8.
  5. Candidate handbook and course brochure for more details.

Domain 1: Fundamental principles and concepts of an information security management system (ISMS) (13 q)

A health app maker must protect patient data under national law. What kind of obligation is this?

⬜ A contractual obligation
⬜ An internal policy
⬜ A legal or regulatory obligation

Which standard in the ISO/IEC 27000 family gives guidance on information security risk management?

⬜ ISO/IEC 27005
⬜ ISO/IEC 27701
⬜ ISO/IEC 27002

Which standard provides the overview and vocabulary for the ISO/IEC 27000 family?

⬜ ISO/IEC 27000
⬜ ISO 19011
⬜ ISO/IEC 27001

What is the difference between a security objective and a control?

⬜ An objective is a result; a control modifies risk
⬜ They mean the same thing
⬜ A control is a result; an objective is a technical tool

What does ISO/IEC 27002 provide for each control that ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A does not?

⬜ A legal obligation to implement it
⬜ Attributes, purpose and implementation guidance
⬜ The certification requirements

An auditor sees control references 5.x, 6.x, 7.x and 8.x in a Statement of Applicability. What do the prefixes show?

⬜ The four Annex A themes
⬜ The order in which controls must be implemented
⬜ The year each control was introduced

Which relationship among information security aspects is correct?

⬜ Vulnerabilities exploit threats in order to protect the assets
⬜ Controls create vulnerabilities to reduce threats
⬜ Threats exploit vulnerabilities, causing impact on assets

A company moves its email to a cloud provider. What remains the company’s responsibility?

⬜ Managing the risks of the outsourced service
⬜ Nothing, because the provider takes over all risk
⬜ Only paying the invoice on time

Which feature distinguishes machine learning from traditional programming?

⬜ It never needs data
⬜ It cannot make errors once it has been trained on enough data
⬜ It learns patterns from data instead of explicit rules

Which of these is a key information security concern specific to outsourcing?

⬜ Less direct control over how data is protected
⬜ The supplier’s office decoration
⬜ Having too many internal controls to maintain in-house

An auditor sees that integrity controls exist for the HR database. What do they protect against?

⬜ Records being unavailable during outages
⬜ Unauthorized or improper modification of records
⬜ Disclosure of records to outsiders

Which statement about ISO/IEC 27001 certification is correct?

⬜ ISO certifies organizations directly
⬜ Certification guarantees the organization will never be breached
⬜ CBs certify an organization’s ISMS, not products

Which ISO/IEC 27000-family standard specifies requirements for bodies that audit and certify ISMSs?

⬜ ISO/IEC 27006-1
⬜ ISO/IEC 27004
⬜ ISO/IEC 27003

What is an integrated management system?

Which clause covers monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation?

⬜ Clause 7.5
⬜ Clause 9.1
⬜ Clause 6.2

Which clause requires the organization to plan, implement and control the processes needed to meet ISMS requirements?

⬜ Clause 4.3
⬜ Clause 8.1
⬜ Clause 7.4

What must happen to the risk assessment when significant changes occur?

⬜ It stays valid until the next certification cycle
⬜ It must be performed again
⬜ It is replaced by the internal audit

Domain 3: Fundamental audit concepts and principles (14 q)

What qualities must audit evidence have to support the auditor’s conclusions?

⬜ It must come only from the auditee’s managers
⬜ It must be in electronic form
⬜ Sufficient and appropriate

A customer audits its software supplier’s security practices. What type of audit is this?

⬜ Third-party audit
⬜ Second-party audit
⬜ First-party audit

Which audit principle requires auditors to report findings truthfully and accurately?

⬜ Fair presentation
⬜ Confidentiality
⬜ Due professional care

Compared with the 2011 edition, which principle did the current ISO 19011 add to the list of auditing principles?

⬜ Risk-based approach
⬜ Confidentiality
⬜ Independence

The certification body plans to send an auditor who was the client’s IT security consultant last year. Which audit principle does this threaten?

⬜ Evidence-based approach
⬜ Independence
⬜ Fair presentation

Which principle requires auditors to protect sensitive information obtained during the audit?

⬜ Risk-based approach
⬜ Confidentiality
⬜ Integrity

An auditor gets an email from the IT manager confirming that backups are tested monthly. What type of evidence is this?

⬜ Confirmative evidence
⬜ Mathematical evidence
⬜ Physical evidence

An auditor recalculates the percentage of laptops with full-disk encryption from the asset list. What type of evidence is this?

⬜ Mathematical (analytical) evidence
⬜ Verbal evidence
⬜ Documentary evidence

An auditor runs a configuration check showing that USB storage is blocked on sampled laptops. What type of evidence is this?

⬜ Verbal evidence
⬜ Technical evidence
⬜ Confirmative evidence

When auditing a process outsourced to a supplier, what should the auditor check?

⬜ Only the supplier’s price list
⬜ Nothing, because outsourced processes are out of scope
⬜ That the organization controls the outsourced process

How can analysis of large datasets help an auditor?

⬜ It removes the need for audit evidence
⬜ It replaces the need for audit criteria and audit planning
⬜ It can test whole populations instead of small samples

What does the evidence-based approach principle mean?

⬜ Audit conclusions rest on verifiable evidence
⬜ The auditor’s experience replaces evidence
⬜ Only documents count as evidence

An auditor learns the auditee must comply with a sector law requiring data to stay in-country. Why does this matter to the audit?

⬜ Auditors may ignore laws in an ISO audit
⬜ The auditor must enforce the law and issue fines for breaches
⬜ Applicable laws are requirements the ISMS must meet

Which statement describes the auditor’s professional responsibility?

⬜ Act ethically, competently and with due care
⬜ Make the audit as quick as possible
⬜ Always find at least one major nonconformity per audit

Domain 4: Preparing an ISO/IEC 27001 audit (12 q)

What is detection risk in auditing?

⬜ The risk that audit procedures miss a significant error
⬜ The risk of errors before any controls
⬜ The risk that the auditee’s controls fail to prevent errors

Which component of audit risk exists before any controls are considered?

⬜ Control risk
⬜ Detection risk
⬜ Inherent risk

What does materiality help an auditor decide?

⬜ Which issues are significant enough to affect conclusions
⬜ Which auditors join the team
⬜ The audit fee

What level of assurance does an audit provide?

⬜ Absolute assurance that no nonconformity exists
⬜ Reasonable assurance, not absolute certainty
⬜ No assurance at all

Who is responsible for preparing the audit plan and leading the audit team?

⬜ The auditee’s top management
⬜ The audit team leader
⬜ The technical expert

What is the role of a technical expert on an audit team?

⬜ To advise under an auditor’s direction
⬜ To approve the certification decision for the client
⬜ To lead the audit team

What are audit criteria?

⬜ The reasons for doing the audit
⬜ The reference requirements evidence is compared against
⬜ The places, processes and time period the audit will cover

How do the ISMS scope and the audit scope relate?

⬜ They must always be identical
⬜ The audit scope can cover all or part of the ISMS scope
⬜ The audit scope may cover areas outside the ISMS for certification

Before accepting an audit, the team leader checks that information, cooperation, time and resources are adequate. What is this?

⬜ Closing the audit
⬜ Issuing the certificate
⬜ Determining audit feasibility

An audit is planned in a country where business meetings follow strict hierarchy. What should the audit team do?

⬜ Consider cultural aspects in planning and communication
⬜ Cancel the audit
⬜ Ignore cultural differences to stay objective and consistent

What should happen during the initial contact with the auditee?

⬜ Confirm communication and arrangements
⬜ Interview frontline staff
⬜ Present the audit findings

What does an audit plan typically include?

⬜ Objectives, scope, criteria, dates, sites and roles
⬜ The auditee’s corrective action plan for previous findings
⬜ The final audit conclusions

Domain 5: Conducting an ISO/IEC 27001 audit (18 q)

What is a main purpose of the opening meeting?

⬜ To present the audit findings and the recommendation
⬜ To decide on certification
⬜ To explain how the audit will be carried out

A stage 1 audit finds the internal audit has not yet been done. What does this indicate?

⬜ The organization may not be ready for stage 2
⬜ Internal audits are optional before certification
⬜ Certification can be granted immediately

During stage 1, what does the auditor review?

⬜ Every control in operation across all sites
⬜ Only the financial statements of the last two years
⬜ The documented ISMS and readiness for stage 2

Stage 1 went well and the team now visits the sites for stage 2. What must stage 2 establish?

⬜ To evaluate the implementation and effectiveness of the ISMS
⬜ To decide whether to accept the audit request
⬜ To train the auditee’s staff

What is the role of a guide during an audit?

⬜ To influence which evidence the auditors collect
⬜ To act as an extra auditor
⬜ To assist the audit team, e.g. with access

An observer from the accreditation body joins the audit. What may the observer do?

⬜ Interview staff and record findings
⬜ Watch without influencing the audit
⬜ Change the audit plan if the client requests it

The auditee strongly disputes a finding during the audit. What should the auditor do first?

⬜ End the audit immediately
⬜ Listen and try to resolve it using the evidence
⬜ Withdraw the finding to avoid conflict with the client

Which method of collecting evidence involves watching how work is actually done?

⬜ Interview
⬜ Document review
⬜ Observation

What does corroboration mean when evaluating audit evidence?

⬜ Accepting the first answer given
⬜ Confirming evidence from one source with another
⬜ Deleting conflicting evidence

What is a disadvantage of relying too much on audit checklists?

⬜ Auditors may miss issues outside the list
⬜ Checklists make audits impossible to plan
⬜ Checklists are forbidden by ISO 19011

An auditor picks the 10 highest-risk change requests to test, based on experience. What sampling method is this?

⬜ Judgment-based (non-statistical) sampling
⬜ Census
⬜ Statistical random sampling

What is the main advantage of statistical sampling?

⬜ It always needs fewer samples
⬜ It needs no planning
⬜ It lets the auditor measure sampling risk

What are audit working papers?

⬜ Records of audit work, evidence and conclusions
⬜ The auditee’s policies and procedures under review
⬜ The certificate

What is an audit test plan used for?

⬜ To schedule the auditee’s holidays
⬜ To replace the audit plan
⬜ To set out which controls to test and how

An auditor finds an access review signed off without any evidence that accounts were checked. What should they do?

⬜ Record a major nonconformity immediately
⬜ Ignore it because a signature exists
⬜ Collect more evidence, then evaluate it

How are audit findings produced?

⬜ By asking the auditee which issues it wants reported
⬜ By counting the number of documents
⬜ By evaluating evidence against the audit criteria

During the audit, a serious risk to safety is discovered. What should the team leader do?

⬜ Report it to the client and, if possible, the CB
⬜ Wait for the closing meeting
⬜ Keep it confidential within the audit team until the report

Why are technical verification techniques, such as reviewing firewall configurations, used in ISMS audits?

⬜ To get objective evidence that controls work
⬜ To replace interviews entirely
⬜ To fix configuration errors on the spot during the audit

Domain 6: Closing an ISO/IEC 27001 audit (7 q)

How are audit conclusions reached?

⬜ By following the auditee’s preferences about the outcome
⬜ By considering all findings against the objectives
⬜ By looking only at the last day’s findings

Which is a typical item on the closing meeting agenda?

⬜ Presenting the findings and conclusions
⬜ Selecting the audit team
⬜ Setting the audit criteria

A stage 2 audit finds two minor nonconformities. What may the audit team recommend?

⬜ Certification, once action plans are accepted
⬜ Certification with no follow-up
⬜ Refusal of certification until a new audit is done

How should conclusions be presented to top management?

⬜ In technical jargon so the findings sound precise
⬜ Clearly and objectively, with evidence
⬜ Only positive points

What should an auditor check when evaluating an auditee’s action plan for a nonconformity?

⬜ That it was written by the auditor to save the client time
⬜ Correction, root cause, corrective action, deadlines
⬜ That it blames a specific person

Who makes the certification decision?

⬜ The auditee’s top management
⬜ The audit team leader alone, at the closing meeting
⬜ Staff who did not conduct the audit

What should happen to diverging opinions about findings that remain unresolved at the closing meeting?

⬜ The audit should be repeated from scratch
⬜ They should be recorded
⬜ They should be deleted from the report

Domain 7: Managing an ISO/IEC 27001 audit program (8 q)

A year after certification, auditors return for a shorter audit of selected ISMS processes. Why?

⬜ To issue a new certificate every year after a full re-audit
⬜ To confirm continued conformity between recertifications
⬜ To replace internal audits

When might a certification body suspend a certificate?

⬜ When the auditee hires new staff or changes its managers
⬜ When the auditee changes its logo
⬜ When the ISMS persistently or seriously fails

How does the PDCA cycle apply to managing an audit program?

⬜ It means auditing every process every day
⬜ It applies only to the auditee’s ISMS, never to audit programs
⬜ Plan, audit, monitor and improve the program

Why must audit records be managed carefully?

⬜ So that they can be shared publicly as marketing material
⬜ To show the program ran and protect confidentiality
⬜ Because they replace the certificate

An organization holds ISO 9001 and ISO/IEC 27001 certificates and wants one audit of both. What is this called?

⬜ A joint audit
⬜ A combined audit
⬜ A follow-up audit

How long must a certification body keep the records of a certified client?

⬜ For as long as the client remains certified
⬜ Current cycle plus one full cycle
⬜ Only until the certificate is issued

Six weeks after the audit, the certification body returns only to check the fixes for two nonconformities. What is this visit for?

⬜ To audit a new supplier the client has just contracted
⬜ To start a new certification cycle
⬜ To verify corrective actions and their effectiveness

When does a recertification audit take place?

⬜ Every month
⬜ Before the three-year certificate expires
⬜ After the certificate has already expired
